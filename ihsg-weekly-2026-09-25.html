01 THE WEEK IN BRIEF

A rebound that did not last

The IHSG fell 3.09% as foreign investors sold a net Rp3.16tn of shares over five days, according to IDX. Thursday alone accounted for Rp1.49tn. The index gained 97.868 points on Wednesday, then lost 133.020 points by Friday. The bounce did not hold, and the selling continued.

The Fed raised rates on 16 September, before this trading week, and Bank Indonesia held its rate on 23 September. JISDOR rose 0.97% between the two Fridays, adding to the loss for an investor measuring returns in dollars without a hedge. These events give context, but the releases do not show why the IHSG fell.

How the currency calculation works

Start with a dollar amount W and two rupiah-per-dollar rates, S₀ and S₁. The final dollar amount is W × S₀ × (1 + RIDR) ÷ S₁. Dividing that result by W gives the percentage return shown above. The calculation uses decimal returns.

Foreign selling persisted across all five sessions

IDX reports Rp3,155.08bn of foreign net selling for 21 to 25 September. It was the third straight week of net selling, bringing the three-week total to Rp9.46tn. Foreign investors were net sellers every day, and the five daily figures add up to IDX’s weekly total. Thursday alone made up 47.3% of the week’s net selling. These figures measure share trades by investor classification. They do not measure money leaving Indonesia or how many IHSG points foreign investors caused.

IDX also reports Rp53.66tn of trading on the regular market and Rp6.23tn on the negotiated market. Its foreign net figure does not split purchases or sales between those markets, so these reports cannot tell us how much of the foreign selling happened in each one.

03 SECTORS AND TRADING

Ten sectors fell

Only consumer cyclicals ended the week higher, up 0.02%. That small gain is weak evidence of a broader recovery. Industrials fell 5.48%, infrastructure 4.10% and technology 3.96%. Materials and energy also fell despite a midweek bounce. These returns show where prices moved. They do not show which investors bought or sold.

More shares traded even as trading value fell 21.32%. That fits a shift toward lower-priced shares, lower share prices, or both. It does not tell us why investors traded. To judge a recovery, watch whether trading value rises while foreign selling eases and more sectors join in.

04 BIGGEST MOVERS

Three stocks accounted for a third of the decline

IDX’s index-mover table puts BYAN, BBRI and BMRI together at 66.10 points of the IHSG’s net 199.267-point fall. That is about a third of the decline, before gains in other stocks offset some losses. The company questions below are things to check, not confirmed catalysts or price targets.

BYAN

Check coal prices and cash flow

BYAN fell 12.48% and took 28.09 points off the IHSG, more than any other stock in IDX’s table. To assess a recovery, look for evidence in coal prices, sales volume and cash flow. These market figures do not tell us how those parts of the business are doing.

BBRI

Watch the cost and quality of loans

BBRI fell 4.83% and took 23.33 points off the IHSG. The next question is whether funding costs and provisions leave earnings and returns on equity resilient. IDX’s index figures do not say whether foreign investors sold BBRI shares.

BMRI

Watch funding costs and margins

BMRI fell 4.23% and took 14.68 points off the IHSG. A stronger case would need stable low-cost deposits, lending margins and credit costs, along with a wider market recovery. A lower share price alone does not prove the stock is cheap.

The price changes and IHSG point contributions come from IDX. The business questions above are things to check. This report does not claim a current earnings result, a valuation multiple, foreign selling in any one stock or a confirmed company catalyst.

05 OUTLOOK

What would make a recovery more convincing

Watch 6,200 as a nearby round number and 6,300 as the first sign of recovery. Wednesday’s 6,374.912 close is a tougher test. These levels have not been tested as support or resistance. The 6,374.912 figure is a closing price, not the highest price reached during that day.

Our stance

Stay cautious and add exposure in stages. Wait for more sectors to join a recovery and for daily foreign selling to ease. If you measure returns in dollars without a hedge, check the currency cost against a rate you could actually trade at. A lower index level alone does not make shares good value.
