01 / EXECUTIVE THESIS

A rebound without follow-through

The IHSG lost 3.09% over the week. Wednesday’s 1.56% rebound lifted the index after Bank Indonesia’s rate decision, but Friday’s close fell below Tuesday’s. The decisive observation is the failed recovery: a 97.87-point advance was followed by a 133.02-point decline. One positive session did not repair the week’s price damage. [1] [3] [4]

The Fed’s 16 September hike carried into this week as a tighter external backdrop; BI’s 23 September hold kept currency stability at the centre of domestic policy. Broad declines and reported foreign selling in large banks support a cautious allocation stance. They do not isolate how much of the index loss came from monetary policy, FX expectations or company news. A credible recovery needs improving participation alongside price. [6] [7] [8] [9]

LONGHAND RETURN LAB / HYPOTHETICAL SCENARIOS

What survives after currency conversion?

Start with the IHSG’s weekly price return, then vary the exchange rate. A positive USD/IDR change means a weaker rupiah.

Unhedged USD price return

RUSD = (1 + RIDR) / (1 + ΔUSD/IDR) − 1

Why the formula works, and five FX scenarios

For initial dollar wealth W and USD/IDR rates S₀ and S₁, final dollar wealth is W × S₀ × (1 + RIDR) ÷ S₁. Since S₁/S₀ = 1 + ΔUSD/IDR, dividing final wealth by W gives the formula above. Inputs are decimal returns inside the formula.

03 / SECTOR ROTATION & THEMATIC ENGINE

Participation failed the recovery test

All 11 sectors rose on Wednesday; all declined on Friday. Materials and energy led the rebound, while financials fell least in Friday’s sell-off. These two daily snapshots show changing participation, but cannot establish weekly sector leadership or identify “smart money” accumulation. [4] [6]

The roughly 23% decline in traded value per share is consistent with a lower-priced trading mix, lower prices, or both. It does not measure net investment flows. Our rotation test therefore requires three separate improvements: broader advances, recovering traded value and easing foreign selling in comparable observation windows.

04 / HIGH-CONVICTION STOCK THESIS

Three bank theses to test

The strongest evidence here concerns selling pressure. The investment cases below are conditional; a re-rating needs operating evidence as well as better flows.

BMRI

Funding discipline before multiple expansion

Reported foreign net selling: Rp886.53bn. A constructive thesis needs deposit costs to remain controlled as loans grow. Watch the next results for NIM and credit-cost resilience; slower selling alone would not prove an earnings catalyst.

BBRI

Credit costs must validate the recovery case

Reported foreign net selling: Rp645.12bn. A stronger re-rating case would combine stable funding costs with improving asset quality. Lower provisions could support earnings, but weaker credit performance could offset that benefit.

BBCA

A smaller outflow is still an outflow

Reported foreign net selling: Rp242.36bn. Look for deposit-franchise strength to sustain margins and returns on equity. Smaller nominal selling than peers does not establish accumulation or a valuation discount; the comparison is not normalised for market size.
