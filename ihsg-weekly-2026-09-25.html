01 / EXECUTIVE THESIS

The relief rally failed to change the trend

The IHSG fell 3.09% for the week to 6,241.89. It rebounded 1.56% on Wednesday after Bank Indonesia held rates, but gave back those gains over the next two sessions. The market was pricing a broader challenge than a single domestic policy decision: global rates moved higher, rupiah stability remained a policy priority, and foreign investors continued to reduce equity exposure.

The Federal Reserve raised its target range by 25 basis points to 3.75–4.00% on 16 September, citing elevated inflation. Bank Indonesia held the BI-Rate at 5.75% on 23 September to stabilise the rupiah and support sustainable growth. That combination leaves limited room for domestic easing. Wednesday’s rebound was relief, not confirmation of a trend reversal.

Signal, not noise

Average daily share volume rose 2.04%, but average daily traded value fell 21.32% and trading frequency declined 3.95%. Turnover was thinner as selling broadened. That is not convincing evidence of broad accumulation.

02 / MACRO & CURRENCY TRANSMISSION

Rates set the tone; the rupiah is the transmission channel

Federal Reserve3.75–4.00%+25 bps · 16 Sep
Bank Indonesia5.75%Held · 23 Sep
Market implicationFX firstRupiah stability constrains easing

The Fed’s hike raises the sensitivity of rupiah assets to the dollar and global yields. Rupiah depreciation can reduce dollar-based returns for foreign investors and raise imported-input or foreign-currency funding costs for some companies. The impact varies with each issuer’s balance sheet and pricing power; it is not uniform across sectors.

Foreign selling is concentrated in index heavyweights

Foreign investors recorded net selling of Rp559.16bn on Friday; the reported 2026 year-to-date net sell stood at Rp77.52tn. Through Thursday, reported foreign net selling reached Rp886.53bn in BMRI and Rp242.36bn in BBCA. Selling in large index constituents can weigh disproportionately on the benchmark even when smaller stocks move differently.

A verified weekly net-flow split between the regular and negotiated markets was not available in the sources reviewed. Friday’s market-wide figure and the through-Thursday stock figures above are not added together or presented as a weekly total.

03 / SECTOR ROTATION

The sell-off broadened; no clear safe harbour emerged

Wednesday offered a brief rotation signal: all sectors gained, led by basic materials (+3.47%) and energy (+2.59%). By Friday, every sector had turned lower again. The reversal makes the post-RDG rally look more like position adjustment than a durable shift in capital.

04 / STOCK-SPECIFIC SIGNALS

Three names that reveal the regime shift

BMRI

A test of foreign appetite for large banks

Reported foreign net selling through Thursday reached Rp886.53bn, the largest among the stocks cited by the source. The near-term catalyst is not valuation alone: investors need to see distribution ease and the rupiah stabilise before relying on a re-rating.

BBCA

Franchise quality meets multiple pressure

Reported foreign net selling through Thursday was Rp242.36bn. Franchise quality may support the longer-term thesis, but near-term changes in the rupiah risk premium can weigh on large-cap multiples.

TINS

Relative strength needs fundamental confirmation

TINS gained 3.19% in the week through Thursday, against the IHSG’s direction. That relative strength is notable, but it does not establish a re-rating catalyst. Watch for confirmation from commodity prices and operating performance; the sources reviewed do not verify a specific trigger for the week.

These are tactical zones based on the available closing levels and price response, not the output of a detailed technical model. A close below 6,200 would increase downside risk. A recovery above 6,375 would carry more weight if breadth and turnover improved alongside it.

Events to watch, 28 September–2 October

Bank Indonesia’s calendar lists August 2026 Broad Money Developments and the Q2 2026 International Investment Position for the week beginning 28 September. Both help test domestic liquidity and external exposures. September inflation is listed for the following week on BI’s calendar, not this one.

Tactical stance

Defensive tilt; selective buy on dips. Keep risk in cyclical and high-beta shares measured until selling eases. Staged accumulation near support is more defensible if the rupiah stabilises and market participation improves.
